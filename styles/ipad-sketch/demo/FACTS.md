# Facts — From Cherry to Cup

Every claim in the narration and on the page, with its source and the quoted wording.

| Claim (film) | Source | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee cherries ripen on the branch, from green to red | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffea_arabica | "These are as dark green as the foliage until they begin to ripen"; "maturing bright red to purple"; red or yellow depending on the variety |
| Two seeds inside, face to face ("2 seeds inside") | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_bean | "it most commonly contains two seeds with their flat sides together" |
| — a single round seed (peaberry) is the exception | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaberry | about 5% of harvested beans |
| The cherries dry whole in the sun, then the husk comes off ("dried whole", "hulled") | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_processing | "The whole fruit is cleaned after harvest and then dried in the sun …"; "Once dry, the fruit is held in bulk silos until it goes to the mill for hulling" (drying can take about four weeks) |
| Roasting turns the beans yellow, then brown | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_roasting | "As the coffee absorbs heat, the color shifts to yellow and then to increasingly darker shades of brown." |
| "Cinnamon" between yellow and brown | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_roasting | "cinnamon" names a very light roast level, around first crack; the film uses it as the colour between yellow and brown, slightly before the crack |
| Near 196 °C, they crack, like popcorn ("~196 °C", "first crack") | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_roasting | "At approximately 196 °C (385 °F), the beans will emit a cracking sound." Roaster guides give roughly 196–205 °C, e.g. https://shop.beanmachines.co.uk/blogs/news/what-is-first-crack-and-second-crack-in-coffee-roasting |
| Ground, and met with hot water | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_preparation | brewing: ground roasted beans steeped in or filtered with hot water |
| "the seed of a cherry" / 一颗樱桃的种子 | — | a figure of speech: the coffee fruit is called a "coffee cherry"; it is not the fruit of the cherry tree |
