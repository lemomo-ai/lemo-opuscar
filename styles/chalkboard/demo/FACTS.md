# Facts on the board and in the voice

Checked on Dong Chinese (dong-chinese.com), which quotes the Shuowen and shows historical forms from the Academia Sinica character database (小學堂).

1. **射 is a bow and a hand; later written 身 + 寸.**
   Shuowen, 射: 「弓弩發於身而中於遠也。从矢，从身。射，篆文从寸。寸，法度也，亦手也。」 ("A bow or crossbow is let go from the body and hits far off. From 矢, from 身. In seal script 射 is written with 寸. 寸 means a rule, and also the hand.")
   Etymology on the page: "Depicts a hand (又) pulling back a bowstring (弓) with an arrow (矢) to shoot. Later written as 身 and 寸."
   https://www.dong-chinese.com/wiki/射
   → board: 「身←弓 · 寸←手」, 「弓 + 手」; voice lines 4–6 and 8.
2. **矮 = 矢 (meaning) + 委 (sound).**
   Shuowen Xinfu: 「矮，短人也。从矢，委聲。」 ("矮: a short person. From 矢, with 委 as the sound.") "Phonosemantic compound. 矢 represents the meaning and 委 represents the sound."
   https://www.dong-chinese.com/wiki/矮
   → board: 「矢 + 委 / arrow → length · wei → sound」; voice line 8 (委只管读音).
3. **短: the arrow as the measure of length.**
   Shuowen: 「短，有所長短，以矢為正。从矢，豆聲。」 ("短: where there is long and short, the arrow is the standard. From 矢, with 豆 as the sound.")
   https://www.dong-chinese.com/wiki/短
   → board: 「以矢为正 / the arrow is the ruler」, the arrow laid as a ruler; voice line 7.
4. **The popular claim** (射 = "one inch tall" should mean short; 矮 = "let an arrow go" should mean shoot) is a saying that circulates online. In the film it is the child's question and the first three voice lines, credited to no one. "委 has a sense of letting go" belongs to that claim only.
5. **The four forms of 射** on the timeline are simplified chalk redrawings after the forms shown on the 射 page above: oracle script (c. 1250–1000 BC, a bow with an arrow), bronze script (early Western Zhou, a hand added at the string), seal script as given in the Shuowen (身 + 寸), and regular script. They are teaching sketches, not tracings of a specific inscription.
6. **Stroke order** follows GF 0023-2020 (通用规范汉字笔顺规范).
7. **The date on the board**, 28 October 2026, is a Wednesday.
