## Figures checked and not used

- **First section: 6 km and 1993-05-28, not 4.4 km.** Chinese official and press sources give 6 km and 5 stations for 锦江乐园—徐家汇 (Jinjiang Park–Xujiahui), opened for (sightseeing) trial operation on 1993-05-28 (tickets on sale from 1993-04-09 per 今日闵行, "Minhang Today"). The 4.4 km found in English Wikipedia is a different span, 徐家汇—上海南站 (Xujiahui–Shanghai South Railway Station, four stations). The film's title follows: 《6 公里》.
- **"London took a hundred-odd years to reach 400 km"**: no reliable source gives the year London reached ~400 km. The time line states only what is sourced: London 1863 (6 km) → 2026 (402 km), 163 years; Shanghai 1993 (6 km) → 2010 (420 km), 17 years, → 2026 (906 km).
- **"The world's longest metro network"** is not claimed anywhere on screen: by the Ministry of Transport (Aug 2026) Beijing has 909.0 km and Shanghai 881.5 km (counted differently); Shanghai's own figure is 906 km.

## Counter rule

The route-length drums land only on sourced values (6.0, 16.1, 420.0, 906.0). Between 1995 and 2010, and between 2010 and 2026, the drums spin blurred and settle on the next value, so no unsourced intermediate figure is ever readable. During the first section (1.75–4.35 s) the counter measures the drawn line from 0 to 6.0.

## Line colours and names

Line colours are the commonly published values (official colour names from Shanghai Release: 1 正红 (bright red), 2 绿 (green), 3 黄 (yellow) …; hex values from the community table on en.wikipedia `Module:Adjacent stations/Shanghai Metro`). English station names follow the network's usual signage (上海体育馆 = Shanghai Indoor Stadium, not Shanghai Stadium). The schematic geometry is our own drawing; topology of the main interchanges follows the real network, minor stations are generated ticks.
