# Facts in the voice and on screen

*Follow the Rain.* Every factual claim the voice says (English, with Chinese subtitles) or the screen prints: where it comes from and the source's own words. The voice text is `tts/lines.json` and `scene.js` (lines with their Chinese subtitles); the screen labels are in `main.js` (`BIOMES`, `drawHUD`) and `scene.js`; subtitles in `../watercolor.srt`. Times are subtitle times. Checked on 2026-11-07.

The rainfall gauge and the map bands are **schematic** and say so on screen ("schematic, from long-term averages"; end card "RAINFALL BANDS ARE SCHEMATIC"); the walk is a drawn transect, not a surveyed one.

## Sources

- [S1] DCCEEW (Australian Government), "Outback Australia – the rangelands" — https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/land/rangelands — "Australia is the driest inhabited continent in the world; 70% of it is either arid or semi arid land. The arid zone is defined as areas which receive an average rainfall of 250mm or less."
- [S2] CSIRO, "Beyond drought: How we're combatting our dry continent" (2024) — https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/Articles/2024/June/combat-drought-Australia — "Australia is the driest inhabited continent in the world."
- [S3] Bureau of Meteorology, climate statistics, Alice Springs Airport (015590) — http://www.bom.gov.au/climate/averages/tables/cw_015590.shtml — annual "Mean rainfall (mm) … 283.4"; "Decile 5 (median) rainfall (mm) … 241.7" (1941–2026).
- [S4] NVIS Fact sheet, MVG 20 – Hummock grasslands (Australian Government) — https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/mvg20-nvis-hummock-grasslands.pdf — "hummocks formed by grasses in the genus Triodia"; "Present extent (km²) 1 372 841"; "rainfall varies from 150 mm to 350 mm."
- [S5] Geoscience Australia, Area of Australia — https://www.ga.gov.au/scientific-topics/national-location-information/dimensions/area-of-australia-states-and-territories — "Australia's total area is 7,688,287 square kilometres."
- [S6] NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service, "Mulga" — https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/plants-and-animals/mulga — "The sophisticated arrangement of mulga branches funnels water to the stem and on to its long taproot".
- [S7] ABARES, Australia's forests, Eucalypt forest profile — https://www.agriculture.gov.au/abares/forestsaustralia/profiles/eucalypt-forest — "The term ‘eucalypt’ includes approximately 900 species in the three genera Eucalyptus, Corymbia and Angophora." "Some mountain ash trees can grow to more than 90 metres, making this the tallest plant species in Australia, one of the tallest hardwoods, and one of the tallest flowering plants in the world."
- [S8] ABARES, Forest facts — https://www.agriculture.gov.au/abares/forestsaustralia/forest-facts — "Eucalypt forests covering 101 million hectares (77% of the native forest area)". "The area of Rainforest is 3.4 million hectares (3% of the native forest area)." "Fire is a major component of the ecology of most Australian forests, particularly eucalypt forests."
- [S9] G. E. Burrows, "Epicormic strand structure in *Angophora*, *Eucalyptus* and *Lophostemon* (Myrtaceae): implications for fire resistance and recovery", *New Phytologist* 153: 111–131 (2002) — https://researchoutput.csu.edu.au/en/publications/epicormic-strand-structure-in-angophora-eucalyptus-and-lophostemo/ — in eucalypts bud initiation is greatest "at the level of the vascular cambium, which is protected by the maximum bark thickness".
- [S10] Country Fire Authority (Victoria), "After the fires – how nature recovers" — https://news.cfa.vic.gov.au/news/after-the-fires-how-nature-recovers — "The growth is born from buds that lie dormant beneath the bark, sometimes within only a few weeks of a fire going through."
- [S11] University of Nottingham press release, "Scientists discover, climb and describe the world's tallest tropical tree" (April 2019) — https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/news/pressreleases/2019/apr/tallest-tree.aspx — the yellow meranti "Menara" in Sabah "came in at 100.8 meters, making it also a probably the tallest flowering plant on Earth", "beating the previous record holder: a eucalyptus tree in Tasmania."
- [S12] Australian National Botanic Gardens, *Rainforest – surviving the wet and dark*, teachers' notes (2012) — https://www.anbg.gov.au/gardens/education/programs/pdfs/Rainforest_surviving-teachers-2012.pdf — "Today rainforest covers 20,000 square kilometres, less than 1% of Australia's land surface." "Climatic and geological changes over the past 50 million years have led to the evolution of flora and fauna adapted to drier and more impoverished conditions." "Rainforest was the major vegetation type over much of Australia until the continent drifted sufficiently northwards to allow the Antarctic ocean and associated cold currents to develop (about 30 million years ago). As Australia moved northwards the climate became drier". "Leaf, flower and fruit fossils from 50 million years ago have been found near Adelaide, South Australia, Anglesea in Victoria and Nerriga on the NSW south coast." "As the climate became drier, the frequency of bushfires increased, the rainforest contracted".
- [S13] Wet Tropics Management Authority, "World Heritage Area – facts and figures" — https://www.wettropics.gov.au/world-heritage-area-facts-and-figures.html — "Topaz has an average annual rainfall of 4,423mm over 31 years and Babinda 4,287mm over 100 years."
